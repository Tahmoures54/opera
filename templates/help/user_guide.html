OPERA User Guide

A practical guide to commissioning readiness, evidence and handover.

1. Project setup

Create a project, define systems and subsystems, register equipment and assign project members using OPERA roles. Membership is tenant-scoped and does not alter the inherited account role.

2. Check sheets

Create the applicable Pre-Commissioning, Commissioning or Functional Test check sheet. Define inspection items and acceptance criteria, issue the controlled sheet, record actual results and complete the required QC verification and contractor/client sign-offs. Signed-off revisions are not edited in place.

3. Punch List

Raise A/B/C/D punch items against the correct subsystem or equipment, assign an owner and target date, record corrective evidence and request closure. Independent verification and authorized waivers must retain their audit history.

4. Witness and Hold Points

Schedule the required witness, hold or review point, record notification and attendance, document the result and release the point through the authorized project role. Follow the project's approved inspection and test plan.

5. Readiness milestones and handover

MC — Mechanical Completion
Confirms that construction/mechanical completion evidence and the applicable pre-commissioning prerequisites have been accepted.
RFC — Ready for Commissioning
Confirms that the subsystem is released to enter commissioning under the approved project procedure.
RFSU — Ready for Start-Up
Confirms that commissioning and start-up prerequisites have been met and the system is ready for the start-up authorization process.
Operations Acceptance
Records the authorized Operations acceptance or rejection of the handover package.
Implementation note: In the current OPERA release, the certificate stored internally as type RF is the Ready for Commissioning (RFC) certificate. There is no separate Ready-for-Start-Up certificate type named RF; RFSU is the implemented next milestone. The displayed workflow must not be interpreted as an additional RF stage. Project contracts and approved procedures take precedence if they define these abbreviations differently.

6. Progress and evidence

Progress snapshots and issue-time certificate snapshots preserve the records available at the time of capture. An indicator or snapshot is not a substitute for an authorized certificate, signed dossier or contractual acceptance.

Electronic sign-off in this release is an authenticated acknowledgement, not a qualified cryptographic signature. Contractual authority and acceptance criteria remain governed by approved project procedures.
